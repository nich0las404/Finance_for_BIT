Corporate Valuation & Module Conclusion

Corporate valuation is the ultimate application of everything learned in this module. It answers the fundamental question every investor and manager asks: "What is this business actually worth?"

By combining the Time Value of Money, cash flow forecasting, and the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC), financial analysts can determine a company's intrinsic value and make informed strategic decisions.

The Crown Jewel: Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)

A Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model is the gold standard for corporate valuation. It values a company by projecting its future Free Cash Flows (FCF) and discounting them back to the present day using the company's WACC.

💡 Intrinsic Value = Σ [FCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t] + Terminal Value / (1 + WACC)^n
Where FCF is the cash available to all investors, and WACC is the blended cost of capital.

Module 2 Recap: The Corporate Finance Journey

Let's review the foundational pillars you have successfully mastered in this module:

1. Time Value of Money (TVM): The core principle that a dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow due to opportunity cost and inflation.

2. NPV & IRR: The critical decision-making tools used to evaluate if a specific project creates value (NPV > 0) or meets the required return hurdles (IRR > Hurdle Rate).

3. Capital Structure & Cost of Debt: The strategic mix of debt (which is cheaper and tax-advantaged) and equity (which represents ownership and carries a higher required return).

4. WACC: The blended, after-tax cost of all capital sources, serving as the ultimate hurdle rate and discount rate for the entire firm.
