Sensitivity Analysis & Valuation Stress-Testing

Sensitivity Analysis is a technique that tests how changes in key assumptions impact the final valuation. It provides a range of possible outcomes rather than a single, misleading number.

Since DCF relies heavily on forecasts of growth, margins, and discount rates, sensitivity analysis is essential for understanding uncertainty. It answers the question: "What happens to the valuation if my assumptions are wrong?"

Key Variables to Test

The most sensitive variables are WACC and terminal growth rate, as small changes in either can significantly impact valuation. Revenue growth and operating margins also have substantial effects on projected cash flows.

Analysts typically test a range of values for each variable to understand the full spectrum of possible outcomes. This helps identify which assumptions require the most careful estimation.

📊 Primary Sensitivity Drivers:
• WACC (Discount Rate): Directly dictates the present value discount factor applied to cash flows.
• Terminal Growth Rate (g): Drives the infinite compounding calculation of terminal value.
• Revenue Growth & Operating Margins: Determine cash flow generation capacity.

Why Sensitivity Matters

Sensitivity analysis reveals which assumptions drive valuation, validates your inputs, and supports better decision-making. It builds credibility by acknowledging uncertainty and providing a realistic range of outcomes.

Presenting a valuation range rather than a single number demonstrates thoughtful analysis and protects against overconfidence. Investors and managers rely on this insight to evaluate downside risk and upside potential.

💡 Valuation Credibility & Risk Management:
Single Point Estimate -> Stress-Test -> Realistic Valuation Spectrum (Bear / Base / Bull)

Creating & Interpreting a Sensitivity Table

A sensitivity table maps valuation outcomes across different combinations of WACC and terminal growth rate. It helps visualize the range of possible values and the impact of key assumptions.

For example, a table might show valuation increasing with higher growth and decreasing with higher WACC. This visual tool makes it easy to see which variables have the greatest impact and what the worst-case and best-case scenarios might look like.

Terminal Growth (g) \ WACC WACC 9% WACC 10% WACC 11%
g = 1% $11.5M $10.5M $9.6M
g = 2% $13.8M $12.8M (Base) $11.6M
g = 3% $16.5M $15.2M $13.8M
🔍 Interpreting the Sensitivity Table:
• WACC Impact: As WACC increases (more risk), valuation decreases (moving left to right from $11.5M to $9.6M).
• Growth Impact: As terminal growth increases (more optimism), valuation increases (moving top to bottom from $11.5M to $16.5M).
• Uncertainty Spectrum: Demonstrates a valuation range of $9.6M (Bear) to $16.5M (Bull) around the $12.8M Base Case.
