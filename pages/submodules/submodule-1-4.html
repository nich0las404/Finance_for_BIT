Master how Net Income flows into Retained Equity on the Balance Sheet and tops the Cash Flow Statement under Operating Activities.

The 3 Financial Statements Interconnection

No financial statement exists in isolation. The Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement form an interconnected loop, telling a unified story of your lemonade stand's operational performance, financial position, and physical cash liquidity.

Here is exactly how the three statements link together step-by-step using your Month 1 numbers.

Link 1: Net Profit Drives Retained Earnings & Operating Cash Flow

At the bottom of your Income Statement, you calculate your Net Profit ($126). This bottom-line figure flows directly into both of the other statements:

1. To the Balance Sheet (Equity): Net Profit flows into Retained Earnings in the Shareholders' Equity section.

Retained Earnings = Beginning Retained Earnings ($0) + Net Profit ($126) - Dividends ($0) = $126
2. To the Cash Flow Statement (CFO): Net Profit serves as the starting line for calculating Cash Flow from Operating Activities using the indirect method.

Link 2: Depreciation Connects Net Income, Non-Current Assets, and Cash Flow

During Month 1, you recorded $100 in Depreciation for the wear and tear on your $300 electric lemon squeezer. This non-cash entry ties all three statements together:

1. On the Income Statement: Depreciation appears as an operating expense, reducing Net Profit.
2. On the Balance Sheet: Accumulated Depreciation reduces the Net Book Value of your PP&E under Non-Current Assets from $300 down to $200.
3. On the Cash Flow Statement: Because no physical cash left your register for depreciation, the +$100 is added back to Net Profit in Operating Cash Flow.

Link 3: Working Capital Changes Link Assets/Liabilities to Operating Cash Flow

Operational timing differences tracked on the Balance Sheet adjust the net cash generated on the Cash Flow Statement:

Accounts Receivable ($30 Asset): Your $30 sale on credit to Mrs. Higgins boosted Income Statement revenue, but since cash wasn't collected, it is subtracted (-$30) from Operating Cash Flow.

Inventory ($50 Asset): Cash spent buying unused lemons and sugar sits as an asset on the Balance Sheet and is subtracted (-$50) from Operating Cash Flow.

Accounts Payable ($40 Liability): Credit used to buy ice sits as a short-term bill on the Balance Sheet and is added back (+$40) to Operating Cash Flow because that cash stayed in your register.

Link 4: Capital Expenditures Link Investing Cash Flow to Assets

When you bought the electric squeezer for $300 cash on Day 1:

1. On the Cash Flow Statement: It is recorded as a -$300 cash outflow under Cash Flow from Investing Activities (CapEx).
2. On the Balance Sheet: It creates a new long-term asset under Property, Plant, and Equipment (PP&E) at its original cost of $300 (before Month 1 depreciation).

Link 5: Financing Activities Link Debt/Equity to Cash and Liabilities

Your $500 parent loan to build the stand setup creates a direct bridge:

1. On the Cash Flow Statement: The borrowed money is recorded as a +$500 cash inflow under Cash Flow from Financing Activities. 2. On the Balance Sheet: It creates a corresponding $500 Debt Liability under Liabilities.

Link 6: The Final Cash Closing Loop

The ultimate test of connectivity happens at the very bottom of the Cash Flow Statement:

Net Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow ($186) + Investing Cash Flow (-$300) + Financing Cash Flow ($500) = +$386
Adding the Net Cash Flow (+$386) to your Beginning Cash Balance ($0) yields an Ending Cash Balance of $386.

This exact number flows directly onto the Balance Sheet as your Cash Asset under Current Assets.

Integration Summary

Income Statement -> Balance Sheet: Net Profit ($126) -> Retained Earnings ($126)
Income Statement -> Cash Flow Statement: Net Profit ($126) -> Starting point for Operating Cash Flow
Balance Sheet -> Cash Flow Statement: Working Capital changes (A/R, Inventory, A/P) -> Operating Cash Flow adjustments
Cash Flow Statement -> Balance Sheet: Capital Expenditures (-$300) -> PP&E Asset balance
Cash Flow Statement -> Balance Sheet: Debt Financing (+$500) -> Debt Liability balance
Cash Flow Statement -> Balance Sheet: Final Ending Cash ($386) -> Cash Asset balance
