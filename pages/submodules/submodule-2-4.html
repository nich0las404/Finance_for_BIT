Capital Structure

Capital structure refers to the specific mix of long-term debt and equity a company uses to finance its overall operations, growth, and assets.

Determining the ideal capital structure is one of the CFO's core responsibilities. The objective is to optimize the balance between financial flexibility and cost, maximizing firm value while minimizing the cost of raising capital.

The Two Pillars: Debt vs. Equity

Debt Financing: Borrowed money (e.g., bank loans, corporate bonds) that must be repaid with interest over time.

* Pros: Cheaper than equity; interest payments are tax-deductible (creating a tax shield); original owners retain full ownership control.
* Cons: Requires mandatory fixed periodic payments regardless of business performance; increases financial distress and bankruptcy risk.

💡 Equity Financing: Funds raised by issuing ownership shares in the company (e.g., common stock, preferred stock).
* Pros: No fixed repayment schedule or mandatory interest obligations; provides a safety cushion during economic downturns.
* Cons: Most expensive form of capital; dilutes existing shareholder ownership and voting power.

Core Capital Structure Theories

Modigliani-Miller Theorem (No Taxes): In a perfect market with no taxes or bankruptcy costs, capital structure does not affect firm value.

Modigliani-Miller Theorem (With Taxes): Because interest is tax-deductible, firms should theoretically use 100% debt to maximize the interest tax shield.

Trade-Off Theory: Managers balance the tax benefits of debt against the rising costs of financial distress (bankruptcy risk). The optimal capital structure occurs at the point where the marginal benefit of debt equals its marginal cost.

Pecking Order Theory: Companies prefer internal financing (retained earnings) first, debt second, and issuing new equity as a last resort to avoid signaling negative news to the market.

Key Capital Structure Metrics

Debt-to-Equity Ratio (D/E): Total Debt / Total Equity
* Measures the relative proportion of leverage used to finance assets.

Debt-to-EBITDA: Total Debt / EBITDA
* Evaluates how many years of core operational earnings would be required to pay off all debt.

Interest Coverage Ratio: EBIT / Interest Expense
* Assesses how easily a company can pay interest on its outstanding debt from operating earnings.
